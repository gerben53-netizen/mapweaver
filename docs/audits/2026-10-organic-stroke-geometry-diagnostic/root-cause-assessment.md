# Root cause assessment

## Established versus unresolved

**Established:** broad terminal lobes already exist at stage B, because the mask is the union of full-radius first/last and neighboring footprints. Short strokes expose very little independent body. Neither land booleans nor RDP is the main source of that broad terminal form in these cases.

**Established separately:** deliberately coarse sampling makes visible body scallops. This is distinct from the end-cap issue. At normal density the tested long stroke is connected and its much smaller body shoulders follow the visible footprint overlaps/bends.

**Unresolved:** why polygon-clipping cannot close the output ring for the retained high-density-versus-current difference pair. The reverse error ends at y values differing by about 4.55e-13; this is consistent with a near-coincident numerical issue, but not proof of the underlying library defect. The installed output-ring builder throws when it finds no linked continuation before closing its event chain (`polygon-clipping.cjs.js`, RingOut.factory). Both input masks independently pass raw Union and Subtract against the exact diagnostic rectangle. Saved results validate. Do not generalize those successes into a claim that the library has no production risks.

## Nine research questions

1. **Are lobes present in individual footprints?** Yes. Stage A shows closed radial shapes with curved broad shoulders; B shows their retained full-width terminal extents. Raw-edge attribution finds zero unattributed mask perimeter. For native Add, first/last footprints contribute 180.13 of 236.36 px (76.2%) of the *raw mask* perimeter. For native Remove bay it is 167.02 of 183.61 px (91.0%). These percentages concern B, not the entire final land boundary.
2. **Caps or body?** Both can contain exposed footprint arcs. The short native cases are cap-dominated. On the long 59-sample case start/end account for about 17.3% of B perimeter; the body is continuous with shoulders at turns. In the sharp-turn case the returning segment overlaps the terminal region, leaving only 14.85 px attributable to the final footprint: caps can be masked by the body, not independently defined.
3. **Does spacing cause repeated stamps?** Yes at deliberately low density: 42 px (0.35D), 21 samples. It changes the raw boundary by up to 13.79 px in the vertex metric and creates a visible scalloped chain. Normal 14.4 px / 59 and high 4.8 px / 174 are smoother, but both retain terminal caps. High density also flattens some Rugged detail and grows raw B from 527 to 743 vertices; it is not a free cap fix. The high/current area difference is unavailable due to the preserved failure; the independently measured boundary vertex distance is 4.02 px.
4. **Diameter, angle, seed, speed, curvature?** Diameters 60/120/240 produce valid saved results but differ in effective spatial phase and simplification. Seed 714 changes the long mask by 13,786.14 px² and up to 20.56 px in the vertex metric. The tangent-angle argument is ignored by Rugged; its rotation comes from the seed. Reversing the trajectory changes sampling phase: same 59 samples but 1,105.94 px² / 4.54 px difference. Adding ten event subdivisions per segment with the normal sampler gives exactly the same mask hash and zero difference: more events alone is not the cause. Curvature exposes different sides of overlapping footprints. This does not reconstruct unknown turns lost between pointer events.
5. **Do booleans or normalization create/amplify the lobes?** Union creates the outer coverage envelope from existing edges and hides overlaps. Add attaches that mask to land; Remove makes its inverse an inlet. Junctions can therefore become pinched or merge an island, but the broad terminal form predates C. Normalization of budget-admissible raw per-Area geometry has zero measured boundary/area change. One raw intermediate exceeds the point budget; it is not accepted as a saved Area until the normal simplifier reduces it.
6. **Does simplification affect caps?** Yes at subpixel scale, not enough to explain a 60-pixel-wide lobe. Native cap distances are .223–.230 px; long .478 px. Changing Add's `smoothing` field from off to high leaves B byte-identical and raises RDP tolerance .48→.672, reducing long D from 189 to 156 stored nodes. It is a simplification multiplier here, not the separate Smooth Coast action. Smooth Coast has its own paired Laplacian passes and 3% area guard; it was not substituted for Add/Remove in this audit.
7. **Can two or three cap/transition corrections solve it?** Not established. Removing first/last one, two or three footprints merely relocates the exposed cap and shortens coverage (lost areas 2,080.09 / 5,053.23 / 8,013.09 px²). This falsifies a simple “remove the terminal dab” fix, not every possible cap strategy. A length-aware taper must account for neighboring overlapping footprints, keep the body unchanged and handle short strokes whose cap zones overlap. Its visual gain and safety must be tested rather than assumed.
8. **Would a continuous envelope structurally prevent it?** It can remove discrete stamp transitions if boundary generation is truly continuous. It does not automatically prevent lobes: choosing round caps still produces round caps. It adds offset/self-overlap, variable-width, join and short-stroke decisions; sharp bends can fold, and narrow bridges can disappear. Those costs are design risks, not measured failures of an unimplemented algorithm.
9. **Lowest complexity/regression risk?** A bounded cap-treatment prototype retains existing body sampling, engine and safety checks. Changing global density affects the whole body and introduces an unresolved comparison failure; a continuous envelope touches more geometry cases. The current evidence supports trying targeted caps first, with strict acceptance gates. It does not prove an envelope will never be necessary.

## Why the refinement helped only partially

Higher multiscale amplitudes make individual footprints less circular, so exposed cap shoulders become less regular. They do not change the core operation: every endpoint is still a complete radial footprint and nearby complete footprints cover each other's inlets. More amplitude therefore changes the cap silhouette but does not independently control the cap's longitudinal extent or terminal taper.
